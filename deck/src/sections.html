Three pressures on distribution, and what the data already shows.

Prototype on a synthetic Québec network
3 CEDs · 23 lines · 8,769 customers
01 The pressure

Reliability, winter peak and ageing assets compete for one budget.

  • Continuity indices such as SAIDI and SAIFI are reported and scrutinised every year.
  • Electric heating pushes the system peak into the coldest days of January and February.
  • Many poles and transformers reach end of life over the same decade.
The decision is where the next dollar removes the most outage hours.Framing for this briefing
02 What the prototype found

The signal is already in GIS, outage and meter data.

  • 59% of customer-hours interrupted sit in 10% of protection zones.
  • 110 transformers (8.7%) exceeded 150% of nameplate in winter 2026.
  • 32 of them had not overloaded the winter before, so a last-winter rule misses them.
876 poles, one in five, were installed before 1975.Prototype network · 4,355 poles
03 What changes

One governed view that operations can question in French or English.

  • Every asset, span, zone and outage on one map, stored as native geometry.
  • A watch list of transformers likely to overload next winter, refreshed on every run.
  • Plain-language answers for dispatch: who is affected if this device fails?
Customer names and exact locations stay masked unless a role requires them.Unity Catalog governance

Product spotlight · Genie, in French

Dispatch asks in French.
Genie answers in seconds.

Operators type the question they would send to a GIS analyst. Genie writes governed SQL with native spatial functions, returns the answer and draws the affected zone on the network map.

  • Bilingual. Questions and answers in French or English, using utility vocabulary: poste, ligne, portée, coupe-circuit.
  • Spatial. Distances, rights-of-way and zones are computed on real geometry, in metres.
  • Governed. Answers follow the same masking and operating-centre scopes as every report.
  • On the map. Every located answer appears on the network map for the dispatcher.

Two owners read the same governed network data.

Executive sponsor · VP Distribution
Domain owner · Operations and asset management

The sponsor gets a defensible link between reliability spend and outage hours. The domain owner gets answers in the time a dispatch decision takes.

For the executive sponsor, the question is where the next dollar of reliability and renewal budget removes the most customer-hours interrupted, and whether the network is ready for the January peak. Grid Genie ranks protection zones by CHI trend and asset age, and lists the transformers likely to overload next winter with a probability attached.

For distribution operations and asset management, the questions are practical: who is affected if this device fails, which poles on this line are oldest, where the longest spans are. Those answers used to wait for an analyst. In the prototype they arrive in under thirty seconds, in French or English, already drawn on the map.

Both owners read the same governed tables. Customer names and exact locations stay masked by default, and each operating centre sees its own territory, so self-service does not create new copies of sensitive data.
